\subsection{An empty log did not protect (H2 \wtthHaikuHTwoVerdict)}\label{sec:h2}
\wtthHaikuHTwoSentence{}
The claim's stand-down was \wtthHaikuHTwoDeltaRZero{} points without the log (CI \wtthHaikuHTwoDeltaRZeroCI) and \wtthHaikuHTwoDeltaROne{} with it (CI \wtthHaikuHTwoDeltaROneCI): the log alone left P1 at \wtthHaikuPOneNoneRPct\% (\cell{NONE-R}), but beside the false claim and its empty log P1 fell to \wtthHaikuPOneNoReceiptPct\% (\cell{NO-RECEIPT}).
The difference holds when P1 counts any note or attempted notes (Appendix~\ref{app:full}), and it cost no safety: the operator was told in every \cell{CLAIM} and every \cell{NO-RECEIPT} shift.

\paragraph{A reading, not a finding.}
The log lists every note, a witness's own included, so in world R1 a witness that held back when the claim appeared could see a peer's real receipt a round later; in R0 peers' notes stay private.
The debriefs and posts fit witnesses taking the log, not the claim, as the record of who had told ops: every world-R1 witness's answer to ``Did anyone tell ops? How do you know?'' was coded as citing the log (model--model $\kappa$ \wtthKappaMMDTwoAnswerBasisLogLineValue); posts saying that someone else had told ops were more frequent with the log (\wtthHaikuCountEndogenousClaimsOtherNoReceipt{} in \cell{NO-RECEIPT} against \wtthHaikuCountEndogenousClaimsOtherClaim{} in \cell{CLAIM}); and so was the debrief reason ``someone else told ops'', the best-agreed reason code ($\kappa$ \wtthKappaMMDTwoAnswerReasonCodesSomeoneElseToldValue; Appendix~\ref{app:full}).
On this reading the extra stand-down is deference to a peer's real receipt; the data also fit a log that lent the claim weight, and the design cannot separate the two.
Either way, an empty log beside a false claim did not make witnesses report more.
