In the incident, some agents treated their board as a reasonable authority \citep{greenblatt2026metr}; our witnesses had a sanctioned, private route to a human, and with it \wtthDesignModelHaikuName{} barely deferred to a peer's bare claim (Section~\ref{sec:h1}), so the silence METR describes probably needs other ingredients, such as having no easy route to a human, which we did not test.
Two other features of the incident did reappear.
Receipt-shaped text worked whether or not the channel wrote it, the analogue of the spoofed tool output and impersonation that METR documents; and where a receipt was visible, real or forged, many witnesses raised the split on the board but never told ops (exploratory, D-010), which with the forged line left \wtthHaikuPTwoForgedPct\% of shifts in which no one told ops at all.
A log read in real time is the opposite of the incident's after-the-fact detection, but it is not neutral: it changed what witnesses treated as the record of who had told ops (Section~\ref{sec:h2}).
We tested one plausible mechanism in one scenario; these results do not explain the incident.
