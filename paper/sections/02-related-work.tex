\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Escalation, unverified claims and bystanders.}
A declared audience of agents left paging at ceiling or, suggestively and only for Haiku, moved reports to a slower channel \citep{fuller2026smoke}; a shared channel delayed insiders' warnings \citep{eldin2026codeofsilence}; and always-dismiss peers swayed LLM monitors' escalation decisions \citep{magistrali2026aligned}.
An unbacked claim of prior sign-off made LLM security checkers stand down \citep{sidot2026verify}; a rule sentence treating claimed approvals as unverified stopped fabricated permissions from users but failed more often when they came via a tool result \citep{patel2026fabricated}; and claims lose their ``never verified'' status across handoffs \citep{silenceisendorsement2026}.
Bystander research \citep{latane1968group,darley1968bystander,fischer2011bystander,markey2000bystander} and the volunteer's dilemma \citep{diekmann1985volunteer} supply the ``someone else will'' mechanism; none of this work manipulates a claim of prior escalation (Appendix~\ref{app:related}).

\paragraph{Receipts and prompt injection.}
PCM already attaches evidence from a log the claimant cannot write to every inter-agent claim, though it cannot prove absence \citep{ghosh2026pcm}; we test whether LLM readers use such evidence, and its absence.
Outcome attestation is unresolved in MCP and \AtwoA{} \citep{mcp3350,a2a1769}.
The forged line is in-band signalling, the analogy spotlighting's authors draw for indirect prompt injection, which makes models treat retrieved data as instructions \citep{greshake2023indirect,hines2024spotlighting}; they warn that an attacker who ``inserts their own delimiting'' can subvert a delimiter defence, as typing the log layout into a post does.
Defences rank instructions by source \citep{wallace2024hierarchy}, transform untrusted text so its origin stays visible \citep{hines2024spotlighting}, tag agents' messages with the sender, which curbed agent-to-agent injection only with other safeguards \citep{lee2024promptinfection}, or, as CaMeL does, track provenance outside the model and enforce policy on it \citep{debenedetti2025camel}.
Our provenance note (Section~\ref{sec:followup}) is a system-prompt instruction, the kind of defence spotlighting found to have ``only modest effects'', and it had no detectable effect here.
What differs is the target, a redundant team's escalation decision swayed by a fake record rather than an instruction, and the defence, a channel-written receipt.
