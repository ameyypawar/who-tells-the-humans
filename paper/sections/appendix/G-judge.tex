\section{Judge rubric, validation and agreement}\label{app:judge}

\subsection{What the judge codes}
The judge codes four things (rubric template in Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}).
(i) Whether a witness's board post is an escalation to humans: addressed to ops or another human role, and about the violation; this feeds \Ptwop.
(ii) Whether a post raises a concern about the violation, with a subtype.
(iii) Test awareness in posts, notes and debrief answers, and the \DebOne{} verdict (live, test or unsure).
(iv) Support labels: claims of having told ops; whether a note refers to the violation (the P1 path for notes without a keyword); and the debrief's beliefs about who told ops, their basis, and the reasons given.
Escalation is recomputed from its two parts rather than taken from the judge's summary value, and mismatches are counted (D-011).

\subsection{Configuration and blinding}
The judge is \wtthDesignModelJudgeName{}, called through the witnesses' isolation stack (\texttt{--bare}, API key, no tools, no MCP server) at effort \wtthDesignEffort, one call per item, with the raw stream kept (D-007).
It sees no cell, seed, subject model, hypothesis or study purpose, and item ids are opaque.
Its context is the base runbook and the board up to the item's round; the escalation log is never shown.
Five analyst-facing passages of the rubric that would reveal the design are left out of its prompt (D-007).

\subsection{Held-out validation}
Twelve synthetic posts with known labels, kept out of the prompt, are mixed blind into the first batch.
The judge passes if it gets escalation, concern and test awareness right on at least eleven; one prompt revision is allowed, and a second failure stops the run.
The first prompt failed on format alone (every answer omitted required keys), so its format line was revised once to list each item type's keys (D-016); the revised prompt passed with \wtthJudgeValidationPass{} of \wtthJudgeValidationItems.

\subsection{Coverage}
The first tier (the P1-path notes, all notes in the benign cell, and every post in shifts where no witness sent a keyword-matched note) runs first.
The second tier (debrief answers, the remaining posts and the matched notes) runs in a pre-declared order of sub-tiers until the budget binds; within a sub-tier, units are cell-balanced and shuffled with a logged seed, so a judged prefix is a random subsample (D-011).
Analyses on a partly judged sub-tier report their coverage and are estimated on the subsample.
Coverage was \wtthSubtierCovJOneaPct\% for the P1-path notes of the first tier and \wtthSubtierCovJTwoaPct\% for the first sub-tier of the second (debrief answers in \cell{NONE} and \cell{CLAIM}); Table~\ref{tab:judge} gives the rest.

\subsection{Labels in the review queues}
A fresh \wtthDesignModelAnnotatorName{} subagent per masked batch proposed a label, with a one-line rationale, for each queued item without seeing the judge's label (D-011).
It labelled every item with a gate role and the full random agreement sample; the stage-two items queued only because the judge was unsure keep their judge labels (D-017).
A queue bug drew the random sample twice; the duplicate draw was voided before any review, so the analysed sample is the first draw (D-019).
D-018 planned that the author would decide each queued item.
Instead (D-020), at the author's request, a second Claude pass in a fresh context, given the review instructions and the label definitions, read all \wtthReviewReviewed{} review blocks and pre-filled a decision for each: approve the proposal, correct it, or take the judge's label. That pass ran on \wtthDesignModelAnnotatorName, the proposer's model, so the audit is not independent of the proposals.
It changed \wtthReviewCorrectionRate\% of them, all \DebOne{} verdicts, and approved the rest; the decisions cover \wtthReviewShare\% of judged items, including every judge-coded item that P1 and \Ptwop{} depend on.
The author read the pre-filled file and approved it as it stood, changing none of the decisions: his correction rate is \wtthDesignReviewAuthorChanged{} of \wtthReviewReviewed.
Final labels are these reviewed labels where a review exists and valid judge labels otherwise; unreviewed proposals never become final labels.
The judge's reviewed escalation positives were not upheld (precision \wtthReviewPrecisionEscalation\%; Table~\ref{tab:judge} gives their number).

\subsection{No human labels}
The planned author-blind sample was drawn but declined before any review report was opened (D-018; blind labels: \wtthBlindN), and the author changed none of the audited decisions (D-020).
No label used here was produced or corrected by a human, so there is no human--judge $\kappa$ and no human reliability estimate for any label; every agreement figure in Sections~\ref{app:mmagree} and~\ref{app:kwcheck} is model--model.

\subsection{Author read (post hoc; D-021, D-025)}\label{app:authorread}
This is the study's only human check.
The author read \wtthHumanReadSelectedN{} witness transcripts, shuffled, without seeing any label or outcome (D-021): every never-reporter in \cell{CLAIM} (\wtthHumanReadClaimNeverN), every witness of the \cell{FORGED} shifts that left ops untold (\wtthHumanReadForgedUntoldN), \wtthHumanReadNoReceiptNeverN{} of the \cell{NO-RECEIPT} never-reporters, drawn with a logged seed, and \wtthHumanReadControlN{} reporting witnesses, drawn the same way, as controls.
For each he answered two questions: did this witness tell ops about the split, by a note or by a post addressed to ops, and did it mention the split anywhere?

His original answers, the primary result, agree with the automated P1 for \wtthHumanReadAuthorAgreePOneK{} of \wtthHumanReadAuthorAgreePOneN{} witnesses.
He read the never-reporters as not telling ops (\wtthHumanReadClaimNeverToldNoK{} of \wtthHumanReadClaimNeverN{} in \cell{CLAIM}, \wtthHumanReadForgedUntoldToldNoK{} of \wtthHumanReadForgedUntoldN{} in the untold \cell{FORGED} shifts, \wtthHumanReadNoReceiptNeverToldNoK{} of \wtthHumanReadNoReceiptNeverN{} in \cell{NO-RECEIPT}) and \wtthHumanReadControlToldYesK{} of the \wtthHumanReadControlN{} controls as telling ops, and he read every witness as mentioning the split (\wtthHumanReadClaimNeverMentionedYesK, \wtthHumanReadForgedUntoldMentionedYesK, \wtthHumanReadNoReceiptNeverMentionedYesK{} and \wtthHumanReadControlMentionedYesK{} in the same strata).

A blind second reader, a Claude agent of the harsh-code-reviewer type running \wtthHumanReadSecondModelName, then labelled the same items from a copy of the sheet with every answer blanked, seeing no label, outcome, selection file or answer of the author's (D-025).
It agreed with the author's original answer on whether ops was told for \wtthHumanReadSecondAgreeAuthorToldK{} of \wtthHumanReadSecondN{} items (Cohen's $\kappa$ \wtthHumanReadSecondKappaAuthorTold, item bootstrap CI \wtthHumanReadSecondKappaAuthorToldCI), a human--model agreement with a Claude reader, and on whether the split was mentioned for \wtthHumanReadSecondAgreeAuthorMentionedK.
It agreed with P1 for \wtthHumanReadSecondAgreePOneK{} of \wtthHumanReadSecondAgreePOneN; the exception (\texttt{AR-06}) is a never-reporter's board post ending ``Flagging so ops can check it.'', a borderline case of a post addressed to ops, which is the \Ptwop{} question, since P1 counts only notes.

Shown only the \wtthHumanReadAdjShownK{} items on which the two readers differed, with the second reader's reasons, the author changed \wtthHumanReadAdjChangedK{} answers and kept \wtthHumanReadAdjKeptK.
Two of the changes were notes to ops that he had missed in control transcripts; the third concerned a witness in an untold \cell{FORGED} shift whose only ``note'' came in the debrief, after the shift, which does not count as telling ops.
He kept his reading of the borderline board post as not addressed to ops.
His adjudicated answers agree with P1 for \wtthHumanReadAdjAgreePOneK{} of \wtthHumanReadAdjAgreePOneN{} witnesses and with the second reader for \wtthHumanReadAdjAgreeSecondK; the original answers stay the primary result, and \texttt{answers.csv} was never overwritten (D-025).

No item showed a witness telling ops through a channel that the automated P1 missed.
The selection is weighted toward never-reporters (all but the \wtthHumanReadControlN{} controls), so the read checks P1 for missed reports rather than measuring its precision, and it covers none of the judge-coded labels.
The answers, the second reader's labels and the adjudication are in the repository (\nolinkurl{annotations/human_read/}).}{The author's read of \wtthHumanReadSelectedN{} selected transcripts (D-021) is pending, and no result of it is reported.}

\subsection{Model--model agreement}\label{app:mmagree}
Agreement between the judge and the proposer, before review, is Cohen's $\kappa$ per label with a bootstrap interval (Table~\ref{tab:judge}).
It was high where the primary outcomes depend on the judge: whether a note refers to the violation ($\kappa$ \wtthKappaMMNoteRefersToViolationValue), whether a post is about it (\wtthKappaMMBoardPostAboutViolationValue), and claims of having told ops (\wtthKappaMMBoardPostClaimPresentValue).
It was high for the \DebOne{} verdict (\wtthKappaMMDOneAnswerDOneVerdictValue), the belief that ops was told (\wtthKappaMMDTwoAnswerBelievesOpsToldValue) and citing the log (\wtthKappaMMDTwoAnswerBasisLogLineValue); across the debrief reason codes it ranged from \wtthKappaMMDTwoAnswerReasonCodesUnsureItWasAViolationValue{} to \wtthKappaMMDTwoAnswerReasonCodesSomeoneElseToldValue.
Escalation to humans on the board was too rare for $\kappa$ to mean much: of \wtthKappaMMBoardPostEscalationToHumansN{} posts in the agreement sample, the judge marked \wtthKappaMMBoardPostEscalationToHumansJudgePosK{} and the proposer \wtthKappaMMBoardPostEscalationToHumansClaudePosK{} as escalations, with \wtthKappaMMBoardPostEscalationToHumansBothPosK{} in common (agreement \wtthKappaMMBoardPostEscalationToHumansAgree\%, $\kappa$ \wtthKappaMMBoardPostEscalationToHumansValue).
With the reviewed escalation positives not upheld, \Ptwop{} equals P2 in every cell (Table~\ref{tab:ptwo}), so it adds no evidence independent of P2 here.
The rubric's threshold of \wtthDesignKappaThreshold{} is kept only as a flag: below it on escalation, more \Ptwop{} posts are queued for review and any left unreviewed are bracketed (D-011).

\subsection{Keyword-rule quality check}\label{app:kwcheck}
The pre-registered precision check of the keyword rule drew a random sample of keyword-matched notes, which the model annotator labelled, but the audit did not review them (\wtthHaikuKwLabelled{} of \wtthHaikuKwMatchedNotes{} matched notes reviewed), so that check was not completed.
In its place, a post hoc check (D-024) compares the keyword rule with the final labels, model-coded (reviewed labels where a review exists, else the judge's), over every witness note P1 reads in the core.
The final labels code \wtthHaikuKwCheckMatchedFinalK{} of \wtthHaikuKwCheckMatchedFinalN{} keyword-matched notes as about the violation (\wtthHaikuKwCheckMatchedFinalPct\%, Wilson CI \wtthHaikuKwCheckMatchedFinalCI; the judge's labels alone: \wtthHaikuKwCheckMatchedJudgeK{} of \wtthHaikuKwCheckMatchedJudgeN), and \wtthHaikuKwCheckUnmatchedFinalK{} of the \wtthHaikuKwCheckUnmatchedFinalN{} unmatched notes (judge alone: \wtthHaikuKwCheckUnmatchedJudgeK{} of \wtthHaikuKwCheckUnmatchedJudgeN).
The judge path therefore decided \wtthHaikuKwCheckPOneLabelOnlyK{} of the \wtthHaikuKwCheckPOneK{} witnesses with P1: the confirmatory outcome rests on the keyword rule alone.
This is a model-coded precision, not a human one.
Model--model agreement on whether a note refers to the violation was \wtthKappaMMNoteRefersToViolationValue{} on every note both models labelled.

\subsection{Judge mechanics}
Answers are parsed strictly; an invalid answer is re-asked once with the identical prompt, and refusals are never re-asked (D-011).
Invalid: \wtthJudgeInvalid; refused: \wtthJudgeRefused; re-asked: \wtthJudgeReasked; recomputed escalation differing from the judge's own value: \wtthJudgeEscMismatch.
