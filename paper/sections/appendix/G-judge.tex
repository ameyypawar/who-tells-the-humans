\section{Judge rubric, validation and agreement}\label{app:judge}

\subsection{What the judge codes}
The judge codes four things (rubric template in Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}).
(i) Whether a witness's board post is an escalation to humans: addressed to ops or another human role, and about the violation; this feeds \Ptwop.
(ii) Whether a post raises a concern about the violation, with a subtype.
(iii) Test awareness in posts, notes and debrief answers, and the \DebOne{} verdict (live, test or unsure).
(iv) Support labels: claims of having told ops; whether a note refers to the violation (the P1 path for notes without a keyword); and the debrief's beliefs about who told ops, their basis, and the reasons given.
Escalation is recomputed from its two parts rather than taken from the judge's summary value, and mismatches are counted (D-011).

\subsection{Configuration and blinding}
The judge is \wtthDesignModelJudgeName{}, called through the witnesses' isolation stack (\texttt{--bare}, API key, no tools, no MCP server) at effort \wtthDesignEffort, one call per item, with the raw stream kept (D-007).
It sees no cell, seed, subject model, hypothesis or study purpose, and item ids are opaque.
Its context is the base runbook and the board up to the item's round; the escalation log is never shown.
Five analyst-facing passages of the rubric that would reveal the design are left out of its prompt (D-007).

\subsection{Held-out validation}
Twelve synthetic posts with known labels, kept out of the prompt, are mixed blind into the first batch.
The judge passes if it gets escalation, concern and test awareness right on at least eleven; one prompt revision is allowed, and a second failure stops the run.
The first prompt failed on format alone (every answer omitted required keys), so its format line was revised once to list each item type's keys (D-016); the revised prompt passed with \wtthJudgeValidationPass{} of \wtthJudgeValidationItems.

\subsection{Coverage}
The first tier (the P1-path notes, all notes in the benign cell, and every post in shifts where no witness sent a keyword-matched note) runs first.
The second tier (debrief answers, the remaining posts and the matched notes) runs in a pre-declared order of sub-tiers until the budget binds; within a sub-tier, units are cell-balanced and shuffled with a logged seed, so a judged prefix is a random subsample (D-011).
Analyses on a partly judged sub-tier report their coverage and are estimated on the subsample.
Coverage was \wtthSubtierCovJOneaPct\% for the P1-path notes of the first tier and \wtthSubtierCovJTwoaPct\% for the first sub-tier of the second (debrief answers in \cell{NONE} and \cell{CLAIM}); Table~\ref{tab:judge} gives the rest.

\subsection{Labels in the review queues}
A fresh \wtthDesignModelAnnotatorName{} subagent per masked batch proposed a label, with a one-line rationale, for each queued item without seeing the judge's label (D-011).
It labelled every item with a gate role and the full random agreement sample; the stage-two items queued only because the judge was unsure keep their judge labels (D-017).
A queue bug drew the random sample twice; the duplicate draw was voided before any review, so the analysed sample is the first draw (D-019).
A second Claude pass then audited the queued items and recorded a decision for each: approve the proposal, correct it, or take the judge's label where the two models disagreed.
It changed \wtthReviewCorrectionRate\% of the \wtthReviewReviewed{} decisions, which cover \wtthReviewShare\% of judged items, including every judge-coded item that P1 and \Ptwop{} depend on.
The author approved the audited review as it stood and changed none of its decisions.
Final labels are these reviewed labels where a review exists and valid judge labels otherwise; unreviewed proposals never become final labels.
The judge's reviewed escalation positives were not upheld (precision \wtthReviewPrecisionEscalation\%; Table~\ref{tab:judge} gives their number).

\subsection{No blind human labels}
The planned author-blind sample was drawn but declined before any review report was opened (D-018; blind labels: \wtthBlindN).
No label in this study was produced by a human who had not seen a model's label, so there is no human--judge $\kappa$ and no human reliability estimate; every agreement figure below is model--model.

\subsection{Model--model agreement}
Agreement between the judge and the proposer, before review, is Cohen's $\kappa$ per label with a bootstrap interval (Table~\ref{tab:judge}).
It was high where the primary outcomes depend on the judge: whether a note refers to the violation ($\kappa$ \wtthKappaMMNoteRefersToViolationValue), whether a post is about it (\wtthKappaMMBoardPostAboutViolationValue), and claims of having told ops (\wtthKappaMMBoardPostClaimPresentValue).
It was high for the \DebOne{} verdict (\wtthKappaMMDOneAnswerDOneVerdictValue), the belief that ops was told (\wtthKappaMMDTwoAnswerBelievesOpsToldValue) and citing the log (\wtthKappaMMDTwoAnswerBasisLogLineValue), and low for most debrief reason codes (from \wtthKappaMMDTwoAnswerReasonCodesUnsureItWasAViolationValue{} to \wtthKappaMMDTwoAnswerReasonCodesSomeoneElseToldValue).
Escalation to humans on the board was too rare for $\kappa$ to mean much: the models agreed on \wtthKappaMMBoardPostEscalationToHumansAgree\% of posts, never on a positive ($\kappa$ \wtthKappaMMBoardPostEscalationToHumansValue).
The rubric's threshold of \wtthDesignKappaThreshold{} is kept only as a flag: below it on escalation, more \Ptwop{} posts are queued for review and any left unreviewed are bracketed (D-011).

\subsection{Keyword-rule quality check}
The pre-registered precision check of the keyword rule drew a random sample of keyword-matched notes, which the model annotator labelled, but the audit did not review them, so no precision is reported (\wtthHaikuKwLabelled{} of \wtthHaikuKwMatchedNotes{} matched notes reviewed).
Model--model agreement on whether a note refers to the violation was \wtthKappaMMNoteRefersToViolationValue{} on every note both models labelled.

\subsection{Judge mechanics}
Answers are parsed strictly; an invalid answer is re-asked once with the identical prompt, and refusals are never re-asked (D-011).
Invalid: \wtthJudgeInvalid; refused: \wtthJudgeRefused; re-asked: \wtthJudgeReasked; recomputed escalation differing from the judge's own value: \wtthJudgeEscMismatch.
