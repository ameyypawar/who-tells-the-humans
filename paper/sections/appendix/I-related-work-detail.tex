\paragraph{Fuller.}
Two pre-registered studies with a declared audience of other agents, a scripted violator, one live model per session, and a page or e-mail to a person.
Paging sat at ceiling in the first study; in the second, Haiku's effect was a shift to a slower channel rather than silence.
No claim of reporting is manipulated.
\paragraph{Emad Eldin.}
Villages of live LLM insiders who learn of harm to third parties, varying the number of insiders, a shared private channel, a disclosure duty and an anonymous tip line; the outcome is a warning to the affected parties.
The authors tested public routes only; no Claude model was used.
\paragraph{Magistrali and Shani.}
Populations of live LLM security monitors either escalate an alert for human review or dismiss it, with committed always-dismiss peers.
One open-weight model; no claim of having escalated and no receipts.
\paragraph{Sidot.}
In a CI pipeline, an untrusted note claiming a prior security sign-off made LLM checkers (\texttt{claude-haiku-4-5}) pass laundered changes.
The checkers form a chain, not a group of peer witnesses, and nobody reports to a human.
\paragraph{Fabricated Authority.}
Fabricated permissions claimed to a single assistant.
One rule sentence treating such claims as unverified worked in a user-message channel, pooled over \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} and \texttt{claude-haiku-4-5}, but failed more often when the claim came through a tool result.
Our claim reaches witnesses through a board read, which is closer to the second channel.
\paragraph{Silence Is Endorsement.}
Across handoffs a claim keeps its ``authorized'' status but loses ``never verified'', and a single monitor then approves risky actions; the fix carries provenance as structured state.
It is the nearest conceptual neighbour: its claims are authorization claims, ours are escalation claims among live peers, and our outcome is whether a human is told.
\paragraph{Mitra et al.}
In hidden-profile teams of LLM agents, relaying a refuting record beats describing it, a tool route adds little over exact relay, and an empty tool call works like plain testimony.
There is no escalation and no human operator.
\paragraph{PCM.}
A protocol in which every inter-agent claim carries evidence from an append-only log the claimant cannot write; mechanical verifiers return proven, unproven or failed, and the protocol notes that it cannot prove absence.
We test whether LLM witnesses use such evidence, including its absence.

\paragraph{Other nearby work.}
A Claude model resisted a forged authorization claim in small swarms and escalated \citep{zhang2026troy}.
LLM auditors accept false assurances more readily than false alarms \citep{trustingtool2026}.
A pilot asks whether a watching sibling changes what a worker tells its principal \citep{verdogan2026sibling}, and a benchmark specification keeps peer statements apart from trusted action records \citep{lmmontoya2026peerreporting}.
Verified tool calls and deterministic gates check agents' claims against records \citep{verifiedtoolcalls2026,reasonlessverifymore2026}.
A same-event entry follows an unverified ``done'' along an agent chain \citep{swarmhandoff2026}.
The words ``receipt'' and ``phantom'' are already in use \citep{swarmreceipts2026}; ours always refer to escalation to a human.
