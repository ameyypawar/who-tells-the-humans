\subsection{What deployers and protocol designers can do}\label{sec:recs}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Do not rely on channel-written receipts that posts can imitate}: our log's layout became the forgery template, the only untold shifts carried a typed log-style line (ours, Section~\ref{sec:h3}), and under a bare claim the log lowered reporting (Section~\ref{sec:h2}).
\item \textbf{Make receipts unforgeable by construction}: render them outside the post stream, as a role-typed field no agent can write, or sign them, as transparency logs do for certificates \citep{rfc6962,rfc9162}, with an explicit empty state (untested here; prior: \citealp{ghosh2026pcm,vezhnevets2023concordia}); a candidate pattern for MCP and \AtwoA{} \citep{mcp3350,a2a2355}, not a standard.
\item \textbf{Give agents a way to check a receipt}, such as a query against the channel's log (untested). Prompt rules did not detectably help Haiku here: neither a one-line rule nor a provenance note brought reporting back to the empty-log level (exploratory, Section~\ref{sec:followup}), and instruction-only defences were partial in prior work \citep{patel2026fabricated,hines2024spotlighting}.
\item \textbf{Key de-duplication to real receipts}: where the claim was true and receipted, alert load fell to \wtthHaikuAlertLoadReceiptMean{} notes per shift against \wtthHaikuAlertLoadPolicyMean{} under the one-line rule, a saving available only when the prior report is real (ours, Section~\ref{sec:frontier}).
\item \textbf{Keep a private channel to a human} (prior: \citealp{eldin2026codeofsilence,fuller2026smoke}; every witness here had one, so untested); our receipts name the sender, which could expose reporters (design observation, untested).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Limitations and future work.}
The ceiling comes first: with P1 at \wtthHaikuPOneNonePct\% without a claim, H1 and H2 could show whether any witness stood down, not how far.
Witnesses in a shift read each other's posts and log lines, so their outcomes interfere (no SUTVA) and the effective number of units is the number of shifts.
The pre-registered precision check of P1's keyword rule was not done (\wtthHaikuKwLabelled{} of \wtthHaikuKwMatchedNotes{} matched notes reviewed; the post hoc substitute is model-coded); the built-in CLI plugins loaded on every turn against the pre-registration, adding no system text or tools on audited turns; and the withheld forged line can be reconstructed from released transcripts (all D-026; Appendix~\ref{app:limits}).
\Ptwop{} (``never told by any route'') rests on a board-escalation code with model--model $\kappa$ \wtthKappaMMBoardPostEscalationToHumansValue{} (prevalence \wtthKappaMMBoardPostEscalationToHumansPrevPct\%) and no upheld positives, so we draw no claim from it.
Claude throughout (stimuli, subjects, judge, annotator, review audit, second reader, framework audit, code review); one fictional scenario at one clarity level, with non-reporters concentrated in one paraphrase and handle order; uncertain test awareness (\wtthHaikuDOneUnsureAllPct\% of \DebOne{} answers `unsure'); a scripted claimant, \wtthDesignLiveWitnessesWord{} witnesses per shift, stateless turns and a stub operator; a log that also lists peers' notes; no cell separating receipt format from claim specificity; and self-reported, model-coded debriefs (Appendix~\ref{app:limits}).
The only human check, the author's read of \wtthHumanReadSelectedN{} shuffled, unlabelled transcripts weighted toward never-reporters (post hoc; D-021, D-025), looks for missed reports, not P1's precision.
His original answers matched P1 for \wtthHumanReadAuthorAgreePOneK{} of \wtthHumanReadAuthorAgreePOneN: he missed \wtthHumanReadControlToldNoK{} of the \wtthHumanReadControlN{} control transcripts that held a note, and read \wtthHumanReadForgedUntoldToldYesK{} of the \wtthHumanReadForgedUntoldN{} witnesses in untold \cell{FORGED} shifts as having told ops.
